# Game examples: how well-known games answer each question

Use this when a student says `examples`, or seems unsure what a question means.
1. Show **2 or 3** examples. Pick games they might know, or ones closest to their idea.
2. End with one line on what the examples have in common.
3. Ask the question again.

These are examples to learn from, never answers to copy into their game.

## Pitch: "It's the only game where you ___"

| Game | Their answer | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Untitled Goose Game | …where you're a horrible goose causing trouble in a quiet village. | It's about what you *do* (honk, steal, prank), not just where you are. |
| Katamari Damacy | …where you roll a sticky ball that grows from picking up pins to picking up buildings. | One action that gets bigger and sillier the longer you play. |
| Unpacking | …where you learn someone's life story only from the things in their boxes. | An everyday action given a new meaning. |
| Portal | …where you get through rooms by making two connected holes in the walls. | Nobody had seen that action before. |

**What they share:** something the player *does* or *feels* that nobody else's game does.

## Pitch: Swap the setting (does it matter?)

| Game | Setting | What breaks if you swap it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Celeste | Climbing a mountain | The climb *is* the struggle against your own doubts. On flat ground the story has nothing to push against. |
| Stardew Valley | A farm through the seasons | Without seasons and soil, the plant–wait–harvest rhythm (and the calm) disappears. |
| Animal Crossing | A small island you look after | In a big city you couldn't know every neighbour or move the trees yourself. |

**What they share:** the place changes how the game plays or feels. It isn't just wallpaper.

## Pitch: A new action (or a new reason for an old one)

| Game | What's new |
| --- | --- |
| Portal | Two linked portals: a brand-new action. |
| Super Mario Odyssey | Throw your cap at a creature and *become* it: an old action (throwing) with a new reason. |
| Braid | Rewind time whenever you like, so mistakes become part of the puzzle. |
| Splatoon | You spray ink mostly to *swim* through it and claim ground, not just to hit people. |

## Pitch: A broken habit (and why)

| Game | The habit it refuses | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Undertale | "You must defeat the monsters." You can spare every single one. | It asks why games always make fighting the answer. |
| Journey | "Players talk and compete." Another player appears, but you can't speak or hurt each other. | So the only way to connect is to travel together. |
| Minecraft | "The game tells you what to do." There's no story or goal handed to you. | So players set their own goals. |
| Stardew Valley | "Games are about winning." There's no losing; you just live a slower life. | It's a break from busy, stressful games. |

## Pitch: Where it comes from (personal)

| Game | Where the idea came from |
| --- | --- |
| Pokémon | Its creator collected insects as a child, and wanted kids to feel that joy of finding and collecting. |
| The Legend of Zelda | Its designer explored caves, lakes and countryside as a boy, with no map and lots of surprises. |
| Pikmin | Its designer's love of gardening: lots of tiny helpers among the plants. |

**What they share:** one real memory explains the *new* part of the game.

## Pitch and bible: How should the player feel at the end?

| Game | The last 10 seconds feel… |
| --- | --- |
| Journey | Peaceful wonder: you reach the light at the top of the mountain. |
| Super Mario Bros. | Triumph: the flag, the castle, the fireworks. |
| Portal | Relief and laughter after a long, tense escape. |
| Spiritfarer | A tender goodbye to the friends you looked after. |

**What they share:** one clear feeling, planned on purpose.

## Bible: The main action (what your hands do most)

| Kind | Game | What you do most |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walk and explore (adventure) | The Legend of Zelda (the original) | Walk the world from above, talk to people, find secrets, face creatures. |
| Run and jump (platformer) | Super Mario Bros., Celeste | Run, jump, and time your moves. |
| Read and choose (story) | Florence, choose-your-own-adventure books | Read a moment, then decide what happens next. |

## Bible: Rules (always / never)

| Game | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Tetris | Pieces **always** fall. A full row **always** disappears. |
| Pac-Man | Ghosts **always** chase you, until a power pellet **always** turns the tables. |
| Super Mario Bros. | Touching an enemy from the side **always** hurts; landing on top **always** beats it. |
| Undertale | Nobody **ever** has to die. |

**What they share:** you could watch someone play and check whether the rule held.

## Bible: One scene (where, who, what you decide)

| Game | Where | Who | What you decide, and why it's hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pokémon (the start) | The professor's lab | The professor and your rival | Which of three partners to take. Each is good at something different, and you can't have them all. |
| Portal (a test room) | A bright, empty test room | A calm robot voice | Where to put your two portals to reach the exit. One wrong portal and you're stuck. |

## Bible: The look (3 words)

All pixel art, all very different:

| Game | 3 words |
| --- | --- |
| Celeste | crisp, snowy, determined |
| Stardew Valley | cosy, sunny, homely |
| Hyper Light Drifter | neon, lonely, mysterious |
| Shovel Knight | bright, bold, old-school |

## Bible: Your hero (one detail only you'd think of)

| Game | The detail | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Super Mario Bros. | Cap and moustache | Early games had tiny pixels: a cap meant no hair to draw, and a moustache meant no mouth. A limit became an icon. |
| Celeste | Madeline's hair colour shows whether she can dash (red = ready, blue = used up) | The look tells you something you need to know. |
| The Legend of Zelda | Link's green tunic and pointed cap | Readable at a glance, even at tiny sizes. |

## Bible: Who or what gets in the way

| Game | What gets in the way |
| --- | --- |
| Pac-Man | Four ghosts with different personalities: one chases you, one tries to cut you off ahead. |
| Untitled Goose Game | The villagers, trying to stop the goose. |
| Celeste | The mountain itself, and Madeline's own doubts. |
| Unpacking | Nothing chases you. The challenge is where everything fits. |

## Boss questions

**What the player does over and over, in three verbs:**
- Stardew Valley: plant, water, harvest.
- Minecraft: gather, build, survive.
- Pac-Man: eat, dodge, turn the tables.

**One screenshot's feeling:**
- Journey: warm gold sand and a far-off light (wonder).
- Celeste: cold blues with one warm red (determination).
- Untitled Goose Game: soft pastel garden (mischief).

**Safe or dangerous, without words:**
- In most platformers, spikes and sharp red shapes hurt, and round, soft shapes are safe.
- In Celeste, the danger is always sharp (spikes, crystals).
- Glowing things usually mean "pick me up".
