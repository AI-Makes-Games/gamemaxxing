# Playtest script

## 1. Before

Ask one at a time:

1. "Who's the first kind of person you'd love to play this? For example: someone who never plays games, or someone who loves hard games."
2. "What should they **do** in the first few minutes?"
3. "And how should they **feel**?"
4. The same for a second kind of player.

**Round 2:** first ask "Same two players as last time?" If yes, copy them from `round-1.md` and go straight to watching.

## 2. During (read this to the student)

> Swap seats with your tester. They play, you watch. **Don't help, don't explain, don't point.** If they ask you something, just say "What would you try?"
>
> While you watch, type short notes here, like:
> - "stuck at the door for a minute"
> - "smiled at the dog"
> - "stopped trying to jump the gap"
>
> I'll keep your notes. Type "done" when they finish or after about 10 minutes.

Record each note as a bullet, exactly as typed. Don't reply to notes except a quiet "got it" if they seem unsure you're saving them.

## 3. After

The student asks the tester, one at a time, and types the answers:

1. "What were you trying to do?"
2. "What surprised you?"
3. "Where were you bored or confused?"

## 4. One fix

List the problems you saw in the notes and answers, plainly (for example "the door looked like a wall", "the gap was too far"). Ask: "Which one is the biggest? Let's fix just that one." If there's only one problem, ask instead: "Shall we fix that?"

If a note clashes with one of the student's rules (a tester wanting to undo a choice in a game where choices can't be undone), say so and let the student decide. Their rule usually wins, and the note goes in `design/later.md`.

## `playtest/round-N.md` format

```markdown
# Playtest round N

## Before
- Player 1: <who> should <do>, and feel <feel>.
- Player 2: <who> should <do>, and feel <feel>.

## Notes while watching
- <note>
- ...

## After
- What were you trying to do? <answer>
- What surprised you? <answer>
- Where were you bored or confused? <answer>

## The one fix
<the problem the student picked, in their words>
Fixed: <yes/no, and how, once done>
```
