# The six pitch questions

Ask one at a time. The pitch passes when **3 of the 6** pass. If the idea is mostly "this game, but somewhere else", start with **Personal**.

| Question | Ask | Passes when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only game | "Finish this sentence: it's the only game where you ___." | The blank is something the player **does or feels**, not a place, a look, or just who you are ("drive a bus" is a role; "pick up passengers before your shift ends" is something you do) |
| Swap | "Swap your setting for another one (the moon for Mars, or under the sea). What breaks?" For story or real-life ideas, ask instead: "If your story happened somewhere else, or to someone else, what would be lost?" | Something important breaks: the setting matters to how the game plays or feels |
| New action | "What can players do in your game that the game you love doesn't let them do?" | A new action, or a new reason for an old one |
| Broken habit | "Which habit of games like this does yours refuse to follow?" | They name one habit **and** say why |
| Personal | "Where in your life does this idea come from?" | A real memory, place, person or feeling that explains the **new** part of the idea. "I liked Zelda when my son played it" explains the copied part, so ask once more. |
| Ending | "How should the player feel in the last 10 seconds of your game?" | **One** clear feeling |

## Judging fairly

- Be generous with the student's words but strict about the test. "On the moon" is a setting, not something the player does, so it doesn't pass Only game. "Bounce higher the sadder you feel" does.
- A short answer can pass. "My grandad's allotment" passes Personal.
- If an answer passes a different question than the one you asked, count it for that one too, and don't ask that question later.
- Two feelings ("happy and sad") don't pass Ending yet; ask which one wins.

## Example answers to offer when one doesn't pass yet

Offer two or three like these, clearly marked as examples, never as their answer:

- **Only game:** "...where you win races by slowing down", "...where the enemies are your old diary entries", "...where you can only move when the music plays".
- **Swap:** "On the moon you float, so every jump is a decision you can't undo." "Under the sea you'd have air to manage."
- **New action:** "You can pick up enemies and use them as platforms." "You can rewind, but only once per level."
- **Broken habit:** "No killing: you win by making friends, because I'm tired of games where fighting is the only answer."
- **Personal:** "My first night alone in a new city." "Building blanket forts with my sister."
- **Ending:** "Relieved." "Like they just said goodbye to a friend." "Proud of themselves."
